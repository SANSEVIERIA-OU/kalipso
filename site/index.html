Look at the camera. Kalipso keeps your place.

A teleprompter for your desktop. Put a small reader next to your webcam and speak: Kalipso follows your voice with speech recognition that stays on your computer, scrolls at a steady pace, or waits for your pedal.

Three ways to keep your place

You speak. The script keeps up.

  1. Follow your voice

    Words you have said fade, and the line you are on stays at the same height. Recognition runs on your computer, with Moonshine or Vosk, or the system's own on Windows and macOS.

  2. 321

    Scroll at a steady pace

    It starts at 150 words per minute, a pace drawn from studies of reading aloud; faster or slower while you read. A countdown first, and the time left as you go.

  3. Step by hand

    Arrow keys, a foot pedal, a presentation clicker or your phone. Each key can do what you choose.

Private by design

Your words stay on your computer.

  • No account, no cloud. Scripts are plain text files in a folder on your computer.
  • Speech recognition runs locally. Audio never leaves your computer and nothing is recorded.
  • The phone remote never sees your script. It only sends commands such as start, pause and step, over your own network.
  • Open source under the AGPL. Read the code, build it yourself, check what it does.

What else it does

Made for the way you present.

  • Speaker notes and documents

    Import notes from PowerPoint and Impress, one section per slide, or Word, Writer and Markdown documents.

  • Follows Impress live

    Present with LibreOffice Impress and the reader jumps to the notes of the slide on screen. On Linux, Windows and macOS.

  • Mirror for glass

    Flip the text horizontally or vertically for a beam-splitter teleprompter. The controls stay readable.

  • Easier reading

    Larger or smaller text with A− and A+, any font you have, including ones made for dyslexia, wide line spacing and lines that fade at the edges.

  • Write where you read

    One window: in a large one you write in the reader itself, with buttons for bold, italic, sections to jump to and [cues] like [smile] that are never read. Under the camera, it shows only the reader.

  • How it went

    After a voice session: pace, long pauses and time per section, saved as a report if you want one.

Install

Get Kalipso 2.8.

Free packages for Linux, Windows and macOS on the release page. They are not signed yet, so your system asks you to confirm the first time; SHA256SUMS there lets you check each download.

Linux app

Arch and EndeavourOS · Debian 13, Ubuntu 24.04 or newer · Fedora

The full app: write and read in one window, a script library, imports, a movable and mirrorable reader, phone remote and voice following. Every package also brings the Plasma widget, the GNOME extension and the KWin script.

Arch Linux and EndeavourOS, ready to run (kalipso builds it on your computer):

yay -S kalipso-bin

Debian and Ubuntu, in the folder where you saved the .deb from the release page:

sudo apt install ./kalipso_*_amd64.deb

Fedora, in the folder where you saved the .rpm from the release page:

sudo dnf install ./kalipso-*.x86_64.rpm
Other distributions: build from source

KDE Plasma

Plasma 6 widget

A reader on your desktop or panel, with the scripts you write in the app. It comes with every Linux package of the app; without the app, add the .plasmoid from the release page. Voice following needs the Kalipso companion.

KDE setup guide

GNOME

GNOME Shell 48 and 51 extension

A reader from the top bar, with the scripts you write in the app. It comes with every Linux package of the app: log out and back in, then turn it on in Kalipso's Settings. Without the app, install its .zip from the release page. It declares Shell 48 and 51, the versions it is tested with.

GNOME setup guide

macOS

Apple Silicon · macOS 14.4 or later · experimental

The app, as a .dmg: open it and drag Kalipso to Applications. The first time, choose Open Anyway in System Settings → Privacy & Security. On-device system speech needs macOS 26.

Download for macOS

Windows

Windows 10 or 11, x64 · experimental

The app, as an .msi installer or a portable .zip. If SmartScreen stops it, choose More info, then Run anyway. Voice following uses the speech recognizers installed in Windows.

Before you use it.

Does it work with my call or recording app?

Kalipso is a separate reading window; it does not need to be embedded in a particular meeting app. Window placement and capture behavior vary by desktop. Test your actual call or recording setup before relying on it.

Will others see it when I share my screen?

On Windows the app asks the system to leave the reader out of screen shares and recordings. On KDE Plasma 6.6 or later, the window is left out while it is under the camera. macOS offers no reliable way for an app to do this, so share a single window there. In every case, check the view from another participant, or the recorded video, before your presentation.

Can it read PowerPoint or Impress notes?

Yes. The standalone app imports the speaker notes of .pptx and .odp files, one section per slide. It can also follow a LibreOffice Impress slideshow while you present, moving to the notes of the slide on screen.

Which languages can it follow?

The download catalog has Moonshine models for English, German, Spanish, Japanese and Chinese, and Vosk models for those plus Italian. Recognition quality differs by language and model. Manual and steady reading work in any language. The interface is available in 39 languages.

Is my script saved automatically?

Yes. The standalone app, the Plasma widget and the GNOME extension save your script and reading position on your computer as you type. Nothing is uploaded. Only this browser demo resets when you reload it.

Is it ready for an important live presentation?

Kalipso 2.0 is the first public release. Recognition can still lose your place, and testing on real computers and microphones is still limited. Keep a copy of your notes, learn the manual controls and rehearse on your own hardware first.
