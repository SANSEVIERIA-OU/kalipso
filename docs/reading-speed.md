# The default reading speed

Steady speed starts at **150 words per minute**. Each script keeps its own speed once changed, and **−** and **+** change it in steps of 10 while reading.

## How it was chosen

A teleprompter's steady speed should match a person reading a script aloud to listeners. Two kinds of measurement exist: reading aloud in a laboratory, and speaking from a script to an audience. The default combines them.

1. **Reading aloud, in Italian.** A meta-analysis of 77 studies with 5,965 adults found an average reading-aloud rate of 183 words per minute (SD 25.9, 95% CI 177–189). For Italian it is 182 words per minute (5 studies, 511 people); for English, 190 (27 studies). These are short texts read aloud for a test. The author notes that this rate cannot be kept up for long. (Brysbaert 2019, Table 3 and the per-language table.)
2. **From a laboratory to listeners.** Audiobook narration is the closest measured case of reading a script to listeners. ACX plans on 9,300 words per finished hour, which is 155 words per minute. Against the 190 words per minute of English read aloud, that is a factor of 0.82. Other measurements of speech to an audience fall on both sides:
   - university lectures, about 145 words per minute;
   - BBC radio news, about 168 words per minute;
   - TED talks, about 170 words per minute.
3. **The result.** 182 × 0.82 = 149, rounded to the speed's steps of 10: **150 words per minute**.

Italian is not slower to speak. Italians say more syllables per second than English speakers (6.99 against 6.19 in the same texts read aloud; Pellegrino, Coupé & Marsico 2011). But Italian words have about two syllables each, against about 1.2 in the same English text. So in words per minute, Italian read aloud comes out a little lower (182 against 190).

The previous default, 140, was within the range teleprompter apps use: their defaults go from 130 to 160 words per minute. It was about 6% slower than this estimate, and 23% slower than Italian read aloud.
